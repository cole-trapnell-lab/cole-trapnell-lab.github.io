# Abstract

During embryogenesis, cell types arise in a predictable order because developmental
regulators act sequentially. But how evolutionary changes in morphogenesis reshape the
signaling environments that activate these regulators remains unknown. Across vertebrates,
primitive myeloid cells emerge from bone morphogenetic protein (BMP)-patterned ventral
mesoderm through a conserved regulatory program. Here we show that in the annual
killifish, Nothobranchius furzeri, a vertebrate with highly derived embryogenesis, neutrophils
emerge prior to gastrulation, before ventral mesoderm has formed. Vascular progenitors arise
later from ventral mesoderm, whereas myeloid progenitors are largely absent from this tissue.
BMP inhibition abolishes pre-gastrula neutrophil specification, while disruption of Nodal-
dependent mesendoderm formation does not. These findings reveal that conserved cell type
programs can be redeployed within an altered embryonic architecture.

**One Sentence Summary:** 

Annual killifish specify neutrophils before gastrulation and ventral mesoderm formation, revealing that conserved immune cell programs can be redeployed within an evolutionarily altered embryonic architecture.
